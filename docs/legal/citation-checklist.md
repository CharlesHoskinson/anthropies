## J. NEW — R2 condition doctrine, the deep bench (Fable R2a)

**Confirms and hardens Section C. Adds the Supreme Court authority for a
conditional assignment that never carried title, and closes the anti-forfeiture
escape that Section C only narrowed.**

- [ ] **`NEW` *Merritt Hill Vineyards, Inc. v. Windy Heights Vineyard, Inc.*, 61 N.Y.2d 106, 112–13 (1984)** — obligation made "subject to" fulfillment held a condition because "[n]o words of promise are employed." Quotes Restatement § 224.
- [ ] **`NEW` *Oppenheimer & Co. v. Oppenheim, Appel, Dixon & Co.*, 86 N.Y.2d 685, 690–91 (1995)** — **closes the § 227 escape.** The forfeiture-reducing interpretation "cannot be employed if 'the occurrence of the event as a condition is expressed in unmistakable language.'" Also distinguishes conditions precedent to *performance* from conditions precedent to the *effectiveness of the obligation*, where nothing arises "unless and until the condition occurs."
- [ ] **`NEW` *Miller Music Corp. v. Charles N. Daniels, Inc.*, 362 U.S. 373, 375 (1960)** — **Supreme Court, and it is an assignment, not a licence.** Assignee took nothing "not because the author's assignment is invalid" but because the contingency failed. Kills "those are all licence cases" and kills "retroactive divestment" in one move.
- [ ] **`NEW` 17 U.S.C. § 101** — "transfer of copyright ownership" expressly includes a **"mortgage"** and any **"hypothecation"** — defeasible conveyances. Congress wrote conditional transfer into the definition of transfer.
- [ ] **`NEW` H.R. Rep. No. 94-1476, at 123 (1976)** — discusses copyright mortgage foreclosure
- [ ] **`NEW` *Bitmanagement Software GmbH v. United States*, 989 F.3d 938 (Fed. Cir. 2021)** — license-tracking term "was clearly a condition rather than merely a covenant"; breach was infringement. **Found in an *implied* licence with no conditional wording at all** — a fortiori for express "Subject to." ⚠ **cite without a pin; internal star-page unverified.**
- [ ] **`NEW` 3 Nimmer on Copyright § 10.15[A][2]** — "the rights dependent on satisfaction of that condition have not been effectively granted." Quoted in both *Bitmanagement* and *Graham*, 144 F.3d at 237. **The "never vested" formulation.**
- [ ] **`NEW` *MDY*, 629 F.3d at 940 — the a contrario.** Blizzard lost because "nothing in that section conditions Blizzard's grant of a limited license on players' compliance." The wording *MDY* found missing is the wording Anthropic's grant sentence contains.
- [ ] **`NEW` *MDY*, 629 F.3d at 939** — "if the contract is unambiguous, the court construes it according to its terms"
- [ ] **`NEW` *Diepenbrock v. Luiz*, 159 Cal. 716 (1911)** — rejected an "interesting and ingenious" covenant construction; cited in *Jacobsen* for the California rule
- [ ] **`NEW` Restatement § 229 cmt. a** — quoted in *Oppenheimer* for the unmistakable-language limit
- [ ] **`NEW` 17 U.S.C. § 204(a)** — the Terms are the only writing; no one takes under the instrument while striking the words that qualify it {probe: "Section 204(a) makes the Terms"}
- [ ] **`NEW` Cal. Civ. Code § 1641** — the whole of a contract is taken together
- [ ] **`NEW` *Jacobsen*, 535 F.3d at 1381–83** — deepen: "[t]he choice to exact consideration in the form of compliance … rather than as a dollar-denominated fee, is entitled to no less legal recognition"
